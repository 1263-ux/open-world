# Mechanical hall visual comparison (2026-10-30 UTC)

Actual pixels inspected: official university photograph in a separate reference-only file; then all three final Blender CPU previews, not merely their file names. Front preview is deliberately a cropped central elevation for readability; oblique/top frame the full projected footprint. The dark ground is a preview-only plane, created after GLB export and not part of the asset.

- Retained reference cues: pale gray-stone facade, three lower levels, projecting raised central clock tower, round dark clock with light arched surround, large dark arched portal and ground-floor arched windows, restrained pale-gold linear trim, vertical pilasters and parapet teeth.
- Material simplifications: windows are regularized and represented as flat dark facade panels, not real openings; clock details and portal are geometric approximations. The reference tower has more pronounced corner piers, layered stonework and more complex profile than this simplified tower. Windows and side walls hidden by foliage are inferred. No readable plaques, people, trees, signage, imagery or texture from the photo is copied.
- Actual footprint: OSM feature 182055474 was used unscaled; base-vertex validation matches all 25 source vertices after centroid translation. Trim can project up to roughly 9 cm beyond the outline. Source geometry itself is not a field survey.
- The single photo does not establish facade orientation, width of window bays or heights. +X facing is inferred from the projection of the footprint; 9 m lower volume and 15 m tower are visual-relative display estimates, explicitly not survey or verified actual dimensions. The central facade is recognizable rather than photogrammetric.
- The photo was used for observation only; it is not embedded in this work. Photo redistribution rights were not established. OSM contributor attribution and ODbL compliance should be reviewed at integration; model source and provenance are listed in metadata.

Checks: Blender 4.3.2 built-in exporter completed without images or external buffer. `python verify.py` parses the binary glTF, counts 3,369 triangles and 169,324 bytes, verifies all footprint base vertices, no node transforms, and local height bounds 0–14.95 m. Three preview images are 1200 × 700 CPU Cycles renders, 24 samples; GPU performance and in-game Three.js lighting / picking were not tested here. The first CPU run lacked denoising support and failed; denoising was disabled and subsequent runs succeeded. 4.3 online manual direct fetch was blocked with HTTP 402; an official 3.6 search result and actual installed operator were used instead.
